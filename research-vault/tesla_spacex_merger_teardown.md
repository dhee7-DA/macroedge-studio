## Part 4: The Legal & Delaware Chancery Court Minefield

As an equity analyst dissecting this deal, the legal and regulatory risks are where the transaction faces its greatest hurdles.

### 1. The Conflict of Interest Standard (*Tornetta v. Musk*)
Under Delaware corporate law, any transaction between a company and its controlling shareholder is subject to the **"Entire Fairness"** standard—the most rigorous standard of judicial review:
* In *Tornetta v. Musk (2024)*, Chancellor Kathaleen McCormick rescinded Musk’s $56 billion Tesla compensation package because the board was deemed conflicted and lacked arm's-length negotiation independence.
* To survive legal challenges from institutional asset managers (like Vanguard, BlackRock, and CalPERS), both Tesla and SpaceX must establish **fully independent Special Committees** equipped with separate legal counsel and investment bankers.
* The merger must be subjected to a **"Majority of the Minority"** shareholder vote—meaning non-Musk Tesla retail and institutional shareholders must approve the deal terms independently.

### 2. Valuation Parity & Exchange Ratios
The central financial battle will center on the **exchange ratio**:
* At what valuation is private SpaceX equity converted into publicly tradeable Tesla common shares?
* If SpaceX is valued too aggressively (e.g., above $2.5T), Tesla shareholders will claim dilution.
* If Tesla shares are undervalued, long-time SpaceX employees and private venture backers (Founders Fund, Fidelity) will resist locking their equity into a cyclical automotive multiple.
