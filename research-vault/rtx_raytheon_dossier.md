## 🏛️ 1. Company Overview

RTX Corporation — formerly **Raytheon Technologies** — is one of the world's largest aerospace and defense companies. Formed from the 2020 merger of **United Technologies Corporation (UTC)** and **Raytheon Company**, RTX is now a three-segment industrial titan serving both commercial aviation and global defense markets simultaneously.

> The rebranding from "Raytheon Technologies" to "RTX Corporation" in 2023 was designed to signal a unified identity — not three legacy businesses stapled together, but a single platform where defense systems, aircraft engines, and avionics reinforce each other.

## 🏗️ 2. The Three Segments — SOTP Framework

RTX is best understood through three fundamentally different businesses. Each deserves its own sector multiple.

---

### Segment 1: **Collins Aerospace**
> *The quiet backbone of commercial and military aviation.*

Makes the systems INSIDE aircraft — avionics, interiors, landing systems, aerostructures, communication and navigation systems. Serves commercial OEM (new aircraft) AND the massive aftermarket (MRO, spare parts).

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $28.3B | $30.2B |
| YoY Growth | — | +6.8% |
| Operating Margin | ~15–16% | ~16–17% (expanding) |

**The Aftermarket Flywheel**: Every aircraft Collins equips generates decades of high-margin parts and service revenue. Airlines cannot source parts elsewhere — proprietary systems = captive aftermarket. This is the highest-quality, most predictable revenue stream in the RTX portfolio.

---

### Segment 2: **Pratt & Whitney**
> *The engine builder at the center of a multi-billion dollar crisis.*

Designs and manufactures aircraft engines — the PW1000G Geared Turbofan (GTF) for narrow-body jets (A320neo, A220) and the F135 for the F-35 fighter jet.

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $28.1B | $32.9B |
| YoY Growth | — | +17.3% |

**The GTF Engine Crisis**:
- Rare **powder-metal contamination defect** in nickel-based superalloy turbine disks discovered 2023
- ~**1,200 engines recalled** for inspection and refurbishment globally
- Repair turnaround: 60 days estimated → **300+ days actual** as MRO facilities overwhelmed
- Airlines faced **record-high AOG (Aircraft on Ground)** — schedule cuts, Airbus production delays
- **Airbus launched a formal damages claim** against Pratt & Whitney in March 2026
- Cost to RTX: **billions in airline compensation** already provisioned
- Resolution timeline: Full normalization expected **2027–2028**

**The F135 Opportunity**: Powers the F-35, the most widely ordered fighter jet globally. Enhanced Engine Package (EEP) upgrade contract — worth potentially **$20B+ over program life** — is a locked-in government revenue stream.

---

> *The missile and defense systems powerhouse in a world rearming.*

Missiles, air defense systems, radar, electronic warfare, and command & control. The most geopolitically sensitive and fastest-growing segment.

## 🌍 4. The Geopolitical Thesis

The world is rearming at a pace not seen since the Cold War. RTX sits at the intersection of all three primary demand drivers:

**NATO Rearmament**: Post-Ukraine invasion, NATO members committed to 2% GDP defense spending. Closing the gap from current 1.2–1.5% = sustained years of new Patriot, NASAMS, and Tomahawk procurement.

**Indo-Pacific Deterrence**: Japan, Australia, South Korea, Taiwan rapidly expanding air and missile defense. F-35 engine demand (F135) grows as Indo-Pacific orders accelerate.

**Munitions Replenishment**: The Ukraine conflict consumed decades of stockpiled munitions in 2 years. The US and NATO must rebuild inventories. Patriot interceptors (~$4–6M each), Tomahawks (~$2M each) — replenishment is a **multi-year $100B+ procurement cycle** and RTX is the primary manufacturer for both.

## ⚖️ 5. Bull Case vs Bear Case

### 🟢 Bull Case
- Record $271B backlog provides unprecedented revenue certainty for 3+ years
- FCF inflecting sharply: $4.5B (2024) → $7.9B (2025) → $8.5B (2026E). Trajectory continues as GTF crisis costs roll off
- Defense budgets globally are in a multi-decade upcycle — not a single-cycle spike
- Collins aftermarket is a durable, high-margin compounder as global commercial aviation fleet expands
- FCF buyback + dividend program accelerates per-share value creation as share count shrinks

### 🔴 Bear Case
- At ~$176/share, RTX trades at **32–34x trailing P/E** — above historical median. "Priced for perfection"
- GTF crisis not fully resolved. Airbus's formal damages claim could escalate into material litigation costs
- Fixed-price defense contracts in inflationary environment = margin risk
- Ukraine ceasefire or Pacific de-escalation could reduce urgency behind global defense procurement
- Supply chain constraints limit backlog execution speed — revenue recognition may lag order intake
