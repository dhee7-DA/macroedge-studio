### 1. Executive Summary
In a landmark move mirroring China’s industrial "War-Proofing" strategy, India has operationalized the **SHANTI Act (2025)**, ending the 60-year government monopoly on nuclear power. This legislation paves the way for private sector participation in civil nuclear energy and standardizes the deployment of **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)**. As global energy volatility persists due to the Hormuz blockade, India is positioning nuclear energy as the backbone of its "Energy Sovereignty" by 2047.

### 2. The Legislative Pivot: The SHANTI Act
**Objective**: To deregulate the atomic energy sector and attract global/private capital.
*   **Repeal of Atomic Energy Act (1962)**: The old law restricted all nuclear activity to the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE). The new framework allows private companies to build, own, and operate reactors.
*   **Liability Reform**: Replaces the CLND Act (2010), introducing graded liability caps for suppliers. This resolves the decade-long impasse with international vendors (Westinghouse, GE-Hitachi).
*   **FDI Inclusion**: Provisions for up to **49% Foreign Direct Investment** in civilian joint ventures.

### 3. The "Moat" Strategy: Indigenous SMRs
India is not just importing tech; it is building its own "Nuclear Moat" via modularity:
*   **Bharat Small Modular Reactor (BSMR-200)**: A 220 MWe indigenous design optimized for industrial captive power. Bidding for the first pilot projects is scheduled for mid-2026.
*   **SMR-55**: A 55 MWe high-temperature gas-cooled reactor designed specifically for hydrogen production and heavy industry.
*   **Cost Advantage**: By 2028, India aims to lower the levelized cost of nuclear electricity (LCOE) to ₹4.5/kWh, making it competitive with imported LNG.

### 5. Institutional Significance (The "Why it matters")
*   **Energy Security**: Unlike solar/wind, nuclear provides the 24/7 baseload required for India's 7.5% GDP growth trajectory.
*   **The Dedollarization Angle**: By reducing reliance on imported LNG (priced in USD), India strengthens the Rupee against Hormuz-driven energy shocks.
*   **The "Adani/Reliance" Factor**: Major Indian conglomerates are expected to form "Nuclear Verticals" within the next 18 months, mirroring the "Chaebol" model of South Korea.

### 6. Risk Spectrum
*   **Public Perception**: Despite the Act, local land acquisition for nuclear projects remains a political tinderbox.
*   **Waste Management**: The long-term storage of spent fuel for the new flurry of private reactors lacks a clear localized framework.
*   **Geopolitical Friction**: Rapid nuclear expansion may trigger non-proliferation oversight noise from the NSG (Nuclear Suppliers Group).
